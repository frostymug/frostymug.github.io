Multi-Trip Navigation — 4 divergent directions

Izzy · 2026-07-26 · v0 explore · for the Door County trip (departs ~Aug 2)

The design question

How should Frost Rocks let you live across many trips (≈3–15/year) without disturbing the present-tense capture flow?

The load-bearing insight from our interview: there are two verbs with opposite relationships to time.

  • Capture is present-tense & singular. You only ever capture into the current trip. “Switching trips” is nonsensical at the shutter.
  • Review / prep time-travels & is plural. Revisit any past trip (T3), prep an upcoming one (T1). Here roaming across trips is the core flow.

So the four differ mainly on where trip-roaming lives — and each is judged first on whether it keeps roaming away from the capture path. Trip states used throughout: previous active upcoming.

P1 · my lean
Trip Header Dropdown

Trip name lives in the notebook header; tap it for a quiet drop-down shelf. No new nav slot. Roaming is one deliberate reach.
P2
Trips inside Review

The Review tab is the roam-across-trips surface (Previous / Active / Upcoming). Most literal honoring of the two-verbs split.
P3
Dedicated “Trips” Tab

Fills the reserved 5th nav slot with a Trips bookshelf as a top-level place. Most prominent — and most at odds with “switching isn’t a daily gesture.”
P4
Bookshelf Home

App opens to a shelf of trip “covers”; you step into one. The trip becomes the top-level object. Biggest structural change.

Pros & cons

DirectionProsCons / risksVerb-split fit
P1
Header dropdown
  • Daily experience stays 100% single-trip & calm
  • Zero nav change; smallest build — fits a week easily
  • Trip name always visible = built-in capture guardrail
  • Roaming feels “hidden” — fine now, may be too quiet once T1/T3 are rich
  • Header does double duty (label + control)
 Good. Roaming is off the capture path but shares the notebook surface.
P2
Trips in Review
  • Cleanest conceptual model: capture-side surfaces never mention other trips
  • Gives T1/T3 their natural home for free
  • No new nav slot
  • “Where’s Finland?” may not be obvious (it’s under Review)
  • Overloads “Review” to also mean “trip picker”
 Best. Roaming lives entirely on the review side, by construction.
P3
Trips tab
  • Most discoverable; trips are obviously first-class
  • Uses the already-reserved 5th slot
  • Scales visibly to 15 trips
  • Makes switching feel like an everyday gesture — you said it isn’t
  • Splits “review current” (Review tab) from “roam trips” (Trips tab): two doors
 Weakest. Elevates roaming to peer-of-capture prominence.
P4
Bookshelf home
  • Most emotionally rich: each trip is “a chapter” (matches the T3 payoff we want)
  • Trip-as-object scales best long-term
  • Adds a step to every launch — taxes the daily single-trip life
  • Browsing vs. capturing split gets subtle: inside Finland, capture must still hit Door County
  • Biggest build; most risk for a 1-week window
 Mixed. Powerful, but blurs which trip is “current” if you’re browsing another.

The map, across all four

Map ambition is a separate axis we already resolved: Door County v1 ships the floor — a static, offline field map per trip, auto-selected (same treatment Finland got). Every prototype shows the map scoped to the current trip; the zoomable all-trips world map (MapLibre) is deliberately deferred. So picking a prototype is picking a navigation model, not a map bet.

What’s intentionally fake

Fake trip data (Finland + Door County + your Sep/Oct plans) Static gridded “map” placeholder, not the real field map T1 / T3 prep & consolidation flows (not designed yet — shown only as doors) Capture opens an alert, not the camera Encounter detail / review dialogue not wired No real GPS / offline behavior

These are navigation-model prototypes. Judge where trips live and how it feels to move among them — not pixels or the map art.

React to these anchors

  • FR-MT-02 — Is roaming trips kept far enough from capture in your pick? Does the current trip ever feel ambiguous?
  • FR-MT-03 — Does the “in-between” moment (previous Finland + upcoming Door County at once) feel handled, or do we need more?
  • FR-MT-04 — Is hiding roaming (P1/P2) too quiet, or exactly the calm you want? Is surfacing it (P3/P4) worth the daily cost?
  • FR-MT-05 — Anything about trip states (previous/active/upcoming) you’d change now, before T1/T3 design?

My recommendation: P1 for the Door County build (smallest, calmest, safest for a 1-week window and truest to “switching isn’t a daily gesture”) — with P2’s model as the destination once T1/T3 are real (roaming belongs in review/prep). P1 and P2 are compatible: P1’s header shelf can graduate into P2’s review surface later without a rebuild. P3/P4 are the “bigger swing” options if you want trips to feel first-class now — but they spend build budget and daily attention on a gesture you told me is rare.
